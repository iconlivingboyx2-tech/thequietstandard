Journal III

Walking Without Destination

Some places reveal themselves only to those who are willing to move without urgency.

Walking without destination is not an absence of direction. It is the refusal to demand immediate answers from the world.

Every city carries ideas that cannot be discovered from a map. They exist in the rhythm of footsteps, in changing light, in the distance between buildings, and in the silence that exists before noticing becomes language.

Observation requires time.

The fastest route rarely produces the deepest understanding.

Architecture teaches patience. A building is never understood from a single angle. It reveals itself gradually as one continues to move around it.

The same is true of people, ideas, and our own lives.

Not every walk must end somewhere remarkable.

Sometimes the act of paying attention is the destination itself.
